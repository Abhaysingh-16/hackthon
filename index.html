Student Opportunity Verification Guide

A plain-language manual for independently investigating job offers, scholarships, freelance clients, and competitions.

1

How to Detect Lookalike & Spoofed Domains

Scammers register domains that closely mimic real companies to impersonate recruiters. For instance, if an employer is Acme Corp (acmecorp.com), a scammer might register acmecorp-careers.net or acme-hiringportal.com.

Rule of thumb: Look at the exact characters immediately preceding the top-level domain (.com, .org).
Verification step: Use a public WHOIS lookup tool (such as whois.domaintools.com). If the domain was registered within the past 6 months, it is a massive red flag for a company claiming decades in business.
2

The Advance Equipment Check Scam Explained

This is the #1 scam targeting college students seeking remote jobs. The "employer" sends an electronic or cashier's check for \$2,000–\$5,000 to purchase home office hardware (MacBook, printer) from their "authorized vendor".

The Trap: Under federal banking laws, banks make check funds available in your account within a few days *before* the check actually clears. Weeks later, the fraudulent check bounces, and YOU owe the bank every dollar sent to the "vendor".
Golden rule: Never accept checks to buy equipment. Legitimate companies ship pre-configured laptops directly to your door.
3

Why Telegram & WhatsApp Interviews Are Scam Indicators

Enterprise recruiters never conduct formal hiring interviews via text messages on Telegram, Signal, WhatsApp, or Google Hangouts. Scammers hide behind anonymous chat handles to avoid leaving corporate audit trails.

Verification step: Always insist on a scheduled video interview using enterprise platforms (Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams) sent from an official corporate email address.
4

Legitimate Scholarships Never Charge Application Fees

Any scholarship or grant notifying you that you have won a "guaranteed award", but requiring a "disbursement fee", "tax processing deposit", or "membership charge" is 100% fraudulent.

Remember: Genuine foundations and universities give money to students; they never ask students to pay to unlock an award.
5

Your Campus Career Center: Your Free Shield

When in doubt, forward the email or message to your university career advisor, department chair, or financial aid officer. Campus offices maintain lists of known predatory recruiters targeting university student directories.
